Who wins in the air, and does it last?

A study of 10,327 headed shots and 95,942 aerial duels from StatsBomb open data. It asks what makes a header work in attack and in defense, which players head the ball better than their situation predicts, and which of those differences are real skill.

By Filipe Aliseda, statistics student at the University of Florida. Code on GitHub.

For scouts, in one minute

  1. Scout aerial duels won above expectation. It is a real, repeatable skill.

    A player's number in one half of his matches predicts the other half well, even after height, position, the delivery and the pitch location are accounted for.

    Reliability 0.67 (95% interval 0.59 to 0.74)

  2. Height decides who wins the header, then stops mattering.

    It is the biggest single driver of aerial duels, yet taller players do not score more headed goals than their chances predict.

    Defender wins 76% when 10+ cm taller, 39% when 10+ cm shorter (95% intervals 75 to 77% and 38 to 40%)

  3. Do not judge headed finishing from free data.

    Goals above headed xG show no sign of being a skill, but only 47 players have enough headed shots to test, so a moderate amount of skill cannot be ruled out either.

    Reliability about 0 (95% interval below 0 to 0.53)

Data is mostly the 2015/16 Premier League, La Liga, Serie A and Ligue 1. Player rankings with ranges are in chapter 5.

Why headers

Headers decide a large share of set pieces, and clubs pay for players who are good in the air. Yet the usual numbers, like aerial duel win rate, mix skill with situation. A tall centre back who mostly contests long goal kicks will win a lot of duels without being special. This project compares every header with what an average player would have done in the same spot, then checks whether the difference holds up.
