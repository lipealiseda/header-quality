Chapter 1

What free data can measure

Before building any model, every factor on the wish list was checked against what free data actually contains. Some can be measured directly, some only through a stand in, and some not at all.

Where the data comes from

StatsBomb open data is the backbone. It logs every on ball action in 2,651 men's matches, including which body part touched the ball. For every shot it also records a freeze frame with the position of every visible player and the goalkeeper. A smaller set of 351 men's matches has 360 frames, which show nearby players for every event.

Transfermarkt supplies player height, through a free dataset that is updated every season. StatsBomb has no physical data at all, so this join is what makes height part of the story.

Metrica Sports shares two anonymous games of tracking data, with every player's position 25 times a second. It is far too small for the main models, but it allows one test of running speed before a jump.

EA FC ratings from the FIFA video games give jumping, strength and speed scores. They are opinions from EA's scouts, so they are used only in one side test and never inside the main models.

What the final dataset holds

Defensive headers75,968Who got the next ball, and whether the opponent shot within 10 seconds

About 6 in 10 headed shots come from the 2015/16 Premier League, La Liga, Serie A and Ligue 1, the only full club seasons in the open data. Other seasons mostly follow one team, usually Barcelona, and tournaments give each player seven games at most. Seasons that follow one team hold 18% of the aerial duels. The Method page shows the rankings barely change without them.

The duel table has 95,942 duels but the duel model uses 93,684. The other 2,258 had no pass in the 6 seconds before, such as a loose ball, so there is no way to tell which player was defending.

Matching heights to players

StatsBomb and Transfermarkt share no player ID, so the only link is the name. Names are messier than they look.

Two players called Konstantinos Manolas

Transfermarkt lists two Greek defenders with exactly this name, born in 1991 and 1993, measured at 185 and 183 cm. Matching by name alone would have to guess. The matcher also checks that a player's birth year fits the seasons he played and whether he appeared for the same clubs. Only one Manolas played for Roma, so he is the one used.

A nickname that points to the wrong man

The Portugal midfielder Maniche is recorded under his legal name, Nuno Ricardo de Oliveira Ribeiro. An early version matched him to a different player called Ricardo Ribeiro, because those two words appear inside the long name. The fix was a rule that a player known by a nickname cannot be matched on part of his legal name.

A missing height is better than a wrong one. When the matcher is unsure, it leaves the height blank.

In the end, 90% of headed shots and 86% of aerial duels (both players) have a height. Random samples of matches were checked by hand, and goalkeepers average 189 cm against 181 cm for outfield players, as expected.

Licences

StatsBomb allows free use for research and requires its logo on published analysis, but it does not allow the raw data to be shared. The code therefore downloads the data instead of storing it. Transfermarkt's dataset is public domain (CC0), Metrica asks for credit, and EA ratings are scraped from SoFIFA, so they are used but never republished.
