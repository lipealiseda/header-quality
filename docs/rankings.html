Chapter 5

Player rankings

Every player with at least 100 aerial duels and a known height, ranked by duels won above what the model expected. This is the one header metric that passed the reliability test in chapter 4.

How to read the table

Above expected is extra duels won per 100, after shrinking toward average. The model behind it already knows both players' heights, positions, the delivery and the location, so a high number means a player wins more than his size and situation explain.

The 95% range shows where his true number probably sits. It comes from the same statistical model as the shrinkage. When the whole range sits on one side of the middle line, the player is clearly above or below average. Of 228 defenders, 18 are clearly above and 8 clearly below. The rest cannot be told apart from average with this much data.

The rank range answers a related question. If you replayed the season many times with each player's true ability drawn from his range, where would he finish? The top defender, Konstantinos Manolas, could be anywhere from 1st to 32nd. Players close together in the table usually cannot be ordered with any confidence.

The ranges treat the spread of skill between players as known, so they are a little too narrow for players near the 100 duel minimum. Most duels come from the 2015/16 season, so clubs reflect that time.
