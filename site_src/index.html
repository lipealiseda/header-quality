Who wins in the air, and does it last?

A study of 10,327 headed shots and 95,942 aerial duels from StatsBomb open data. It asks what makes a header work in attack and in defense, which players head the ball better than their situation predicts, and which of those differences are real skill.

By Filipe Aliseda, statistics student at the University of Florida. Code on GitHub.

Three things this project found

  • 39% vs 76%How often the defending player wins an aerial duel when he is more than 10 cm shorter than the attacker, against more than 10 cm taller. Height mostly decides who reaches the ball first.
  • 0.67 reliabilityWinning more aerial duels than the model expects is a real skill. It repeats from one set of matches to the next (95% interval 0.59 to 0.74), even after both players' heights are taken into account.
  • No clear signalScoring more headed goals than expected (goals minus xG) shows no sign of being a skill in this data. With only 47 players to test, the data cannot rule out a moderate amount either, so headed finishing stays an open question.

Why headers

Headers decide a large share of set pieces, and clubs pay for players who are good in the air. Yet the usual numbers, like aerial duel win rate, mix skill with situation. A tall centre back who mostly contests long goal kicks will win a lot of duels without being special. This project compares every header with what an average player would have done in the same spot, then checks whether the difference holds up.
